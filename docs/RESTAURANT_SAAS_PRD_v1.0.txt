1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY AND PRODUCT VISION
---------------------------------------

The Restaurant SaaS Platform is a multi-tenant operating platform for restaurants and cafes. It gives diners a QR-based digital menu and ordering experience, gives restaurant teams coordinated kitchen/reception/waiter workflows, and gives owners a central place to manage menus, tables, offers, staff access and customer engagement. A platform-level Super Admin console enables the company to onboard and support multiple restaurant businesses from one shared SaaS platform.

[TABLE 4]
| Product vision / Make restaurant ordering and day-to-day service more accurate, transparent and engaging, while making onboarding and administration simple enough for restaurants with limited technical capability. |

The product is not intended to replace every POS, attendance, payroll or inventory application at launch. It will provide a reliable core ordering and service workflow first, then connect to third-party operational systems through a controlled integration layer.

[TABLE 5]
| Product principle | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Simple for diners | Scan a table QR and order in the browser; account creation is optional. |
| Operational clarity | One persisted order record is the source of truth for kitchen, reception, waiter and customer views. |
| Tenant isolation | Every restaurant business has isolated data, settings, users and feature entitlements. |
| Configurable, not code-editable | Owners can change approved content blocks and offers without writing code. |
| Modular expansion | Games, CRM, integrations and inventory are optional capabilities and must not destabilize core ordering. |

2. PROBLEM STATEMENT AND OBJECTIVES
-----------------------------------

2.1 Problems addressed
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
- Verbal order-taking can create misunderstandings about items, quantities, modifiers and special instructions.
- Customers often have limited visibility into whether an order was accepted, how long it may take, or whether it is ready.
- Kitchen, reception and waiter teams may rely on disconnected communication channels or handwritten tickets.
- Owners may need to visit tables or ask staff to communicate offers manually.
- Restaurant onboarding onto a digital system can be difficult if every venue requires custom development or a separate deployment.
- Existing attendance, POS and inventory systems vary, so forcing every restaurant to replace its current tools would create adoption friction.
- Waiting time can be used for optional customer engagement through single-player or group games and restaurant-funded rewards.

2.2 Business objectives
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
- Reduce order-entry ambiguity by making the customer-selected cart the structured order source.
- Reduce time spent relaying orders between reception and kitchen.
- Give diners clear order status and chef-confirmed estimated preparation time.
- Make offers and approved front-end content changes self-service for owners.
- Provide a repeatable tenant onboarding process with standard configuration and optional custom domains.
- Create a modular commercial model where engagement features such as games can be packaged separately.
- Allow future integration of attendance, payroll, POS and inventory data without coupling the core product to one provider.

2.3 Product success definition
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The first production release is successful when a restaurant can be onboarded, configure its menu and tables, receive a QR order, process it through kitchen and service, create a bill, record its own payment, and update offers without requiring code changes or platform engineering intervention.

3. PRODUCT SCOPE AND RELEASE STRATEGY
-------------------------------------

3.1 In-scope capabilities
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

[TABLE 6]
| Capability area | Product scope |
| --- | --- |
| Customer experience | QR scan, digital menu, item options, cart, order confirmation, status and ETA. |
| Restaurant operations | Kitchen order display, reception order queue, waiter pickup and service status. |
| Restaurant billing | Server-calculated bill, taxes/discounts according to configured rules, QR payment instructions and staff payment verification. |
| Owner controls | Menu, tables, QR codes, offers, branch settings, team access and permitted content blocks. |
| Platform administration | Tenant onboarding, owner invitation, domain mapping, plan assignment, feature access, suspension and audit. |
| Engagement | Optional games, multiplayer sessions, leaderboard and restaurant-funded rewards. |
| Operational extensions | CRM, attendance/POS integrations, inventory and payroll-related data preparation in later releases. |
| Online delivery channels | Receive and process supported third-party marketplace orders (for example Zomato and Swiggy) through a unified restaurant operations dashboard, subject to provider approval and connector availability. |

3.2 Out of scope for the initial core release
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
- Replacing a restaurant’s existing UPI, banking, POS, attendance or payroll provider.
- Processing diners’ food-order payments through the SaaS company’s payment gateway.
- Building a general-purpose website builder that allows arbitrary HTML, CSS or JavaScript execution.
- Automated payroll compliance or tax/legal calculations without confirmed jurisdiction, policy and provider requirements.
- Complex real-time action games, voice chat or open customer-to-customer messaging.
- Automatic attendance integration with every vendor; only providers with documented APIs, webhooks or export formats can be integrated.
- Guaranteed direct integration with every food-delivery marketplace before the platform has obtained the required commercial approval, technical access and provider certification.
- Web scraping, browser automation or unofficial credential sharing as a substitute for an approved food-delivery integration.

4.2 Permission model
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The product shall use role-based access control (RBAC) with permission keys and branch scope. Default roles are provided, but tenant owners may create or customize roles within limits set by the platform. A user may hold different roles at different branches. Platform Super Admin permissions are separate from tenant staff roles.

[TABLE 9]
| Role example | Allowed actions | Explicitly excluded by default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Receptionist | View orders; create/close restaurant bill; record payment; verify payment; view tables. | Edit menu prices; change staff permissions; access other tenants. |
| Chef | View kitchen tickets; accept ticket; set ETA; update preparing/ready status. | View payroll; refund or alter payment records; change menu prices. |
| Waiter | View assigned ready orders; mark picked up/served; view table identifiers. | Change food prices; view tenant billing or subscription settings. |
| Branch manager | Manage assigned branch operations and allowed staff tasks. | Manage unassigned branches unless separately granted. |
| Owner | Manage tenant settings, locations, menus, offers, staff roles and plan-visible features. | Access unrelated tenants or platform secrets. |
| Super Admin | Create/suspend tenants; assign plans; manage domains and platform settings; audited support access. | Unlogged impersonation or routine unrestricted access to customer data. |

5. CORE CUSTOMER AND STAFF JOURNEYS
-----------------------------------

5.1 Customer dine-in journey
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
[IMAGE OMITTED IN TEXT VERSION: Figure 1. Proposed order lifecycle from QR scan through service.]
Figure 1. Proposed order lifecycle from QR scan through service.
1. Customer scans the QR displayed at a table. The platform resolves restaurant, branch and table context from a secure QR token.
1. Customer browses the menu, selects item variants/add-ons, quantity and permitted special instructions.
1. Customer confirms the cart. The server validates menu availability, prices, offers and table context before saving the order.
1. The order appears in the kitchen queue and reception queue. Duplicate submissions must be prevented using an idempotency key.
1. Chef accepts the order, sets an estimated preparation time and updates preparation status.
1. Customer sees the latest status and ETA in the browser. Realtime is an enhancement; a refresh must recover the persisted state.
1. When the chef marks the order ready, the waiter receives a pickup task for table service, or the customer is notified for self-service.
1. Reception generates or reviews the bill. The diner pays directly to the restaurant using its own QR/payment method. Reception verifies the payment and records it.

5.2 Table session and multiple diners
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

[TABLE 10]
| Recommended V1 default - requires confirmation / Use a table session to group dining activity. Each confirmed customer submission remains a distinct kitchen order ticket linked to that table session. Reception can generate a consolidated table bill. Individual split bills can be added as a later capability. Do not make one diner’s draft cart editable by every other diner by default. |

5.3 Staff operational journey
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
- Reception sees incoming orders and table context; it does not need to re-enter the customer’s selected line items.
- Kitchen sees a kitchen ticket (KOT) with items, modifiers, quantities, notes, elapsed time and priority.
- Chef updates order preparation state and supplies an ETA. ETA changes are recorded with actor and timestamp.
- Waiter sees only tasks permitted for their assigned branch/section and records pickup and service completion.
- Reception and kitchen staff use one branch-level operations interface for both in-house orders and supported online marketplace orders.

5.3A Third-party food-delivery order journey
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
- A customer places an order on a supported marketplace such as Zomato or Swiggy.
- The marketplace sends the order to the platform through an approved POS integration, webhook/API connection or an authorized order-aggregation provider.
- The order appears in a unified External Orders queue with marketplace name, external order reference, order timer, line items, modifiers, instructions, amount, delivery/pickup mode and the delivery information permitted by the provider.
- An authorized receptionist/manager reviews and accepts or rejects the order in the platform. The decision is sent back to the originating marketplace through the connector, subject to its supported workflow and deadline.
- Accepted orders are routed to the kitchen as external-channel KOTs. They are clearly labelled with their source and do not require a dine-in table assignment.
- Kitchen staff update preparation status/ETA. The platform forwards supported status changes (for example ready or picked up) to the originating channel.
- Cancellation, customer cancellation, timeout, refund and delivery-partner events are reflected from the source platform when provided. The platform does not independently promise or control marketplace delivery/refund decisions.
- External marketplace orders remain distinguishable from dine-in orders for billing, settlement, reporting and reconciliation.

5.4 Restaurant onboarding journey
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
- Super Admin creates a tenant using restaurant business details; no IP address is required for normal onboarding.
- Platform creates a default branch, tenant slug/subdomain, owner invitation and default configuration.
- Owner completes branding, service mode, menu, tax settings, tables and staff roles.
- Platform generates a unique QR for each table and provides a printable QR export.
- A test order and staff notification check are completed before the tenant is activated.
- A custom domain can be added later through DNS ownership verification and domain mapping.

7. BUSINESS RULES AND OPERATIONAL POLICIES
------------------------------------------

[TABLE 20]
| Rule ID | Business rule |
| --- | --- |
| BR-01 | Tenant means the restaurant business or chain. A location/branch is an operating outlet within that tenant. |
| BR-02 | Every tenant-owned record must be associated with a tenant. Branch-operational records must also identify their location. |
| BR-03 | A table QR identifies tenant, branch and table context; it does not grant staff privileges. |
| BR-04 | Customer order prices, tax, discounts and totals are calculated or validated server-side. Persist a price/tax/discount snapshot at order time. |
| BR-05 | Order status and payment status are separate state machines. |
| BR-06 | Order states: PLACED, ACCEPTED, PREPARING, READY, SERVED, COMPLETED; terminal/exception states include REJECTED and CANCELLED. Allowed transitions are enforced by the backend. |
| BR-07 | Restaurant bill payment states: UNPAID, PAYMENT_REPORTED (optional), VERIFIED_PAID, PARTIALLY_PAID (future), REFUNDED/VOIDED where applicable. |
| BR-08 | Static UPI QR payment is not automatically confirmed by the platform. A staff member verifies receipt using the restaurant’s bank/UPI channel. |
| BR-09 | The company’s payment gateway processes SaaS subscription charges only; restaurant food payments are outside that gateway flow. |
| BR-10 | A table session may contain multiple submitted order tickets. Consolidated bill is the recommended initial behavior; split billing remains a separate decision/feature. |
| BR-11 | Offers, discounts and rewards are revalidated at the backend at the time of application/redemption. |
| BR-12 | Subscription entitlements are evaluated on the server and must not rely on frontend visibility alone. |
| BR-13 | Owner content editing is schema-driven and limited to approved blocks; no arbitrary code execution is allowed. |
| BR-14 | External integrations must preserve source references, sync timestamps and error logs. Unsupported systems use documented CSV/manual fallback. |
| BR-15 | All operational timestamps are stored in UTC and displayed in the branch’s configured timezone. |

| Availability | Initial production target: 99.5% monthly availability for core ordering APIs, excluding approved maintenance and third-party outages. |
| API latency | Target p95 under 500 ms for ordinary read APIs and under 800 ms for ordinary write APIs under agreed load; excludes external provider latency and large exports. |
| Realtime delivery | Target p95 under 2 seconds for in-app order events under agreed load; persisted state remains correct even if realtime delivery is delayed. |
| Durability | Order, order-item, status and bill records must be committed transactionally. No order should depend on a browser-only or websocket-only state. |
| Tenant isolation | Cross-tenant read/write access must be denied at API and database policy layers; automated negative tests are mandatory. |
| Scalability | Initial load-test profile to be agreed; suggested test scenario: 100 tenants and 1,000 concurrent customer/staff sessions, then increase based on observed bottlenecks. |
| Usability | Customer flow must be mobile-first. Staff dashboards must work on common tablets and desktop screens. |
| Accessibility | Target WCAG 2.1 AA for core flows where practicable; keyboard operation and semantic controls required. |
| Security | TLS in transit; secrets server-side; least privilege; audit of privileged actions; rate limits and input validation. |
| Recovery | Proposed initial recovery objectives: RPO <= 24 hours and RTO <= 4 hours, subject to selected Supabase plan, backup design and tested restoration process. |
| Compatibility | Support current evergreen Chrome, Edge, Safari and Firefox versions; exact browser/device matrix to be finalized for QA. |
| Localization | Initial currency, language and tax configuration must be tenant/branch configurable; default market assumptions require business confirmation. |

10. DEPENDENCIES, RISKS AND ASSUMPTIONS
---------------------------------------

10.1 Dependencies
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
- Restaurant-provided menu, pricing, tax settings, branch details, table layout, logo and UPI QR/payment details.
- Company subscription payment gateway account, merchant onboarding and verified webhook configuration.
- Supabase project, hosting, domain/DNS and secrets-management environments.
- Documented APIs, webhooks or export samples for each attendance/POS/inventory provider selected for integration.
- Commercial and technical approval, credentials, sandbox/test access, outlet mapping and certification requirements for each food-delivery marketplace or authorized aggregator connector.
- Restaurant staff training and operating policy for order acceptance, payment verification, cancellation and refunds.

10.2 Risks and mitigations
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

[TABLE 24]
| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Static UPI QR does not prove payment | Unpaid order could be marked paid incorrectly. | Manual verification by authorized staff; payment status separate; audit verifier and timestamp. |
| Tenant data leakage | Critical security and trust issue. | Tenant context enforcement, RLS, least privilege, cross-tenant automated tests and audit. |
| Realtime outage/disconnect | Staff or customer may not see an update immediately. | Persist events/state; reconnect fetch; retry/outbox; visible stale/offline state. |
| Third-party integration variability | Connector development may be delayed or provider API may change. | Adapter pattern, connector certification, versioned mappings and CSV fallback. |
| Marketplace integration approval/eligibility | Direct POS access may have minimum-volume, uptime, support, legal or certification prerequisites; a new SaaS may not qualify immediately. | Validate requirements early; evaluate authorized aggregation middleware; do not promise a marketplace until access and outlet mapping are confirmed. |
| External order acknowledgement timeout | Marketplace may automatically reject or cancel an order if the restaurant does not respond within its deadline. | Prominent timers, sound/visual alerts, retry/reconciliation logic and clear connection-health status. |
| External menu mapping mismatch | Incorrect item/modifier/price mapping may cause failed or incorrect orders. | Explicit mapping review, validation, versioning and controlled menu publishing; keep provider menu sync optional until approved. |
| Feature creep | Core ordering delivery becomes delayed. | Phase gates; keep games, inventory and payroll modular. |
| Rewards abuse | Financial/discount leakage and customer disputes. | Server-side eligibility, quotas, cooldowns, one-time redemption and audit trail. |
| Custom domain complexity | Misrouting or certificate failures. | DNS ownership verification, mapping lifecycle, SSL automation and fallback subdomain. |

10.3 Assumptions
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
- The platform is initially designed for dine-in restaurants and cafes in a single initial market; jurisdiction-specific tax, privacy and invoicing details remain to be confirmed. It may also receive external marketplace orders where an approved connector is available.
- Each tenant can operate one or more branches.
- Customer ordering is browser-based and does not require installation or mandatory login.
- Restaurant customer payment is directed to the restaurant using its own UPI QR/payment instructions; staff manually verifies receipt in the initial release.
- The SaaS company controls the subscription gateway account and receives subscription payments.
- Plans and prices are not finalized; feature-entitlement infrastructure will be built so packaging can change without schema redesign.
- Backend framework is not yet approved; the TRD provides a framework-neutral design with NestJS and FastAPI implementation profiles.

12. GLOSSARY
------------

[TABLE 26]
| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
